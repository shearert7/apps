What is happening here?

{acidSlider === 0 ? (

Temperature Effect: The autoionization of water (H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻) is an endothermic process. As you increase the temperature, Le Chatelier's Principle states the system will shift right to absorb the heat.

This generates more H⁺ and OH⁻ ions equally. Because [H⁺] increases, the pH drops below 7.0, but the water remains completely neutral because [H⁺] still perfectly equals [OH⁻].

) : (

Adding Acid & Le Chatelier's Principle: When you add an acid (like HCl), you introduce a massive spike of H⁺ ions into the system.

Kw = [H⁺] × [OH⁻] = Constant (at {temperature}°C)

Because the equilibrium constant (Kw) must remain exactly the same at any specific temperature, a large increase in [H⁺] means the [OH⁻] must decrease proportionally so their product remains constant.

How it happens chemically: According to Le Chatelier's principle, the system opposes the disturbance. The newly added excess H⁺ ions react with the available OH⁻ ions, shifting the equilibrium to the left (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O). This consumes the OH⁻ ions, drastically driving their concentration down to near zero.
