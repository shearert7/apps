2 · Build the electron configuration

Aufbau principle

Electrons occupy available lower-energy orbitals before higher-energy orbitals in the usual filling model.

1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p
Hund’s rule

Orbitals of equal energy are occupied singly with parallel spins before electrons pair.

[↑] [↑] [↑] → [↑↓] [↑] [↑]
Pauli exclusion principle

Each orbital holds a maximum of two electrons, which must have opposite spins.

[↑↓] allowed · [↑↑] not allowed
This is a teaching sequence for ground-state configurations, not a physical animation of an atom being assembled. Relative orbital energies depend on the atom and its occupancy.

7 · Check your understanding

Explain why nitrogen has three unpaired 2p electrons.

Its 2p³ configuration places one electron in each of the three equal-energy 2p orbitals with parallel spins, in accordance with Hund’s rule.

Determine the full and condensed electron configurations of Ca²⁺.

1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶; [Ar]. Two 4s electrons are removed from neutral calcium.

Identify the exceptions to the usual Aufbau sequence among elements up to krypton.

Cr: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹; Cu: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.

Explain why the p-block is six columns wide.

A p subshell contains three orbitals, each able to accommodate two electrons, giving six electrons in total.

Discriminate between an electron shell, subshell and orbital.

A shell groups orbitals with the same principal quantum number n. A subshell groups orbitals with the same n and orbital type. An orbital is a one-electron quantum state, represented here by its spatial probability distribution.
