Exact 2025 subject matter and school objective codes

1.1.3.6 — Analyse flame tests and atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS) to identify elements and determine the concentration of metallic ions in solution.

1.1.3.2 — Analyse mass spectrometry spectra, to determine the isotopic composition of elements, the relative atomic mass of an element and percentage abundances of the isotopes of an element.

1.1.1.8 — Explain how successive ionisation energy data is related to the electron configuration of an atom.

1.1.4.8 — Analyse data for atomic radii, valencies, ionic radii, 1st ionisation energy and electronegativities to determine periodic trends, patterns and relationships.

1.1.1.6 — Determine full and condensed electron configurations for atoms and ions up to Z = 36, e.g. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵ and [Ne]3s² 3p⁵.

1.1.2.4 — Explain that the relative atomic mass of an element is the ratio of the weighted average mass per atom of the naturally occurring form of the element to 1/12 the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

Codes from the school's 2025 objectives workbook; exact subject matter wording from QCAA Chemistry 2025 v1.3 (January 2026).

Dataset provenance and scientific conventions

AAS: zinc standards from the existing historical AAS prototype; copper standards from the supplied FIA1 Data Sets.xlsx workbook. Synthetic copper data are explicitly labelled. The x-axis extends beyond the highest standard to permit extrapolation. The student can click multiple segments or draw freehand, including the incorrect connect-the-dots approach.

Mass: natural isotope abundance datasets are supplied as rounded reference examples; these are not randomly perturbed and all abundances sum to 100%. Relative atomic mass is calculated from the supplied mass numbers as approximate isotopic masses. The default elements have at least three isotopes. Lead and strontium remain available as additional historical alternatives, although the heaviest and most abundant isotopes coincide for those examples.

Periodic: atomic radii come from the supplied school's 36-element dataset; first ionisation energy and electronegativity from the QCAA Chemistry 2025 v1.3 formula and data book; successive ionisation energy examples from published reference tables. Missing values are hidden from the student graph but retained in teacher answers. Zero electronegativities for He, Ne and Ar are an explicitly labelled classroom plotting convention.

Year 11 scope and cognitive alignment

Unit 1 Topic 1 only: no ppm-to-µg L⁻¹ conversions, molarity, dilution calculations or Unit 2 solution calculations in the active core questions. Relative atomic mass uses isotope masses and percentage abundances, not molarity.

The 30:30:40 balance is an approximation adapted from the senior data-test approach. QCAA does not impose the Unit 3 IA1 marking allocation on a school-devised Unit 1 formative FIA1. The graph construction step is included as working, while AO evidence is evaluated through its associated determination, analysis and interpretation.

Question verbs and cognitive classifications are provisional assessment-design decisions. The final formal paper must be checked against school reporting standards and the evidence elicited by each question.

Print, randomisation and limitations

Print from the generated page using Print student or Print answers. The student printout suppresses model solutions and any interactive curve displayed on screen; students can draw the curve on the printed grid. Teacher answers include calculation working and justified predictions.

Seeded versions are reproducible for generated AAS measurements and balanced question selections. Published periodic data and natural isotope abundances are never silently changed. Automatic assessment of student-drawn AAS curves is formative feedback only, not a final QCAA criterion judgment. The page does not save responses between sessions.
