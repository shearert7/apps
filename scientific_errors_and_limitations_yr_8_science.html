Key Inquiry Concepts: Errors vs. Limitations

Read the definitions carefully before starting the diagnostic check.

Random Errors

Unpredictable variations in measurement that cause readings to scatter above and below the true value.

Examples: Human reaction time on stopwatches, parallax error when reading a scale from an angle, sudden wind gusts.
Systematic Errors

Consistent errors that shift all measurements in the same direction away from the true value every time.

Examples: A scale not zeroed properly (zero error), a ruler with a worn-off end, thermometer calibrated incorrectly.
Method Limitations

Flaws in how the experiment is designed, equipment limits, or variables that were not controlled properly.

Examples: Too small sample size, measuring heat loss without beaker insulation, inconsistent stirring speed.

Stage 1: Guided Walkthrough (I DO)

Scenario 1: Water Heating Experiment

Teacher Mode Active
Experimental Setup: Students heated 100 mL of water in an open glass beaker using a Bunsen burner. They recorded the temperature using a glass liquid thermometer every minute.

Identified Limitations & Errors (Click to explore):

1. Heat Loss (Method Limitation) Uninsulated

An open glass beaker loses thermal energy to the surrounding air. This means the water takes longer to heat than expected.

2. Thermometer Bulb Position (Systematic Error) Placement Error

If the thermometer rests on the bottom of the beaker, it measures the direct temperature of the heated glass rather than the liquid water!

3. Eye Level / Meniscus (Random Error) Parallax Error

Reading the thermometer scale from slightly above or below eye level causes minor random fluctuation in recorded values.
